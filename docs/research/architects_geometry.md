# Geometry strategies for view-optimised residential towers: architects, tokens, critique rubric, city notes

Research date: 2026-09-26. Sources are public web pages, gathered through Composio search (Exa) and page fetches. Tags used:
- **[S]**: directly supported by the cited source.
- **[H]**: a heuristic or engineering-judgement range added for parameterisation. No source states it, so treat it as a tunable default.
- **[U]**: uncertain, or the source is weak (marketing, forum or aggregator). Verify before relying on it.

### Charles Correa: Kanchanjunga Apartments, Mumbai (1970–83)
- **Problem [S]:** in Bombay, an east–west orientation catches the sea breeze and the best views (Arabian Sea and harbour). Those are also the directions of hot sun and monsoon rain.
- **Move [S]:** 32 luxury apartments of four types (3–6 bedrooms) interlocking in split section.
  - The corners are cut away to make **double-height terrace gardens**, a vertical reinterpretation of the bungalow verandah that gives "two lines of defence".
  - Shear end walls carry the cantilevers.
  - Tower proportion is 1:4 (21 m square by 84 m high).
- **Optimises:** view and breeze with rain and sun protection, plus cross-ventilation through the split sections.
- **Source:** https://charlescorreafoundation.org/2022/03/13/kanchanjunga/

### SOM
- **Move [S]:** helical twist; each identical plate turns 1.2° relative to the floor below, 90° in total.
- **Optimises:** confuses wind vortex shedding and spreads marina and Gulf views across units.
- **Example:** Cayan Tower, Dubai.
- **Source:** https://www.som.com/projects/cayan-tower/

### KPF
- **Moves [S]:** chamfered corners for column-free corner rooms and unobstructed views (Madison Square Park Tower); folded, faceted planes (Brooklyn Point).
- **Sources:** https://www.kpf.com/project/45-east-22nd-street ; https://www.kpf.com/project/brooklyn-point
- **[U]** The strength of the "chamfer widens views" claim comes from a secondary site: https://nycinteriordesign.com/buildings/the-madison-square-park-tower

### Foster + Partners: 30 St Mary Axe (office, London)
- **Move [S]:** circular plan with six triangular lightwells. The plan **rotates 5° per floor**, so the lightwells spiral; aerodynamic taper.
- **Optimises:** daylight, natural ventilation driven by pressure differences, lower downdraught at street level.
- **Sources:** https://www.fosterandpartners.com/news/30-st-mary-axe-london ; https://www.harvarddesignmagazine.org/articles/30-st-mary-axe/

### Herzog & de Meuron: 56 Leonard, NYC
- **Move [S]:** 57 storeys of shifted slabs and cantilevers ("Jenga"), designed from the inside out. 145 units, and only 5 are identical. Most units get private outdoor space.
- **Optimises:** a distinct view and terrace for each unit; a high share of corner-like conditions.
- **Sources:** https://www.herzogdemeuron.com/projects/305-56-leonard-street/ ; https://www.architecturalrecord.com/articles/12677-leonard-street-by-herzog-de-meuron

### Rafael Viñoly: 432 Park Avenue, NYC
- **Move [S]:** pure square plan 93 ft on a side; slenderness about 15:1; 10 ft × 10 ft window grid; column-free interiors.
- **Wind [S]:** open double-height mechanical floors let wind pass through; tuned mass dampers.
- **Optimises:** views in all four directions from every floor, and a clear identity.
- **Critique:** part of the Billionaires' Row shadow debate (see C).
- **Sources:** https://www.vinoly.com/works/432-park-avenue/ ; https://www.structuremag.org/article/432-park/

### Jean Nouvel: 53W53, NYC
- **Move [S]:** tapering, faceted form that follows zoning setbacks, with an exposed diagrid.
- **Optimises:** zoning envelope, lateral stiffness, open plans.
- **Sources:** https://www.jeannouvel.com/en/projects/53w53/ ; https://www.structuremag.org/article/53w53-moma-tower/

### SHoP: 111 West 57th, NYC
- **Move [S]:** slenderness about 1:24. Required setbacks multiplied into a "feathered" profile; terracotta pilasters end at each setback.
- **Optimises:** extreme height on a small lot, a legible crown, facade rhythm.
- **Sources:** https://www.shoparc.com/projects/111-west-57-street-2/ ; https://www.archdaily.com/927814/111-west-57th-street-most-slender-skyscraper-in-the-world-tops-out

### Renzo Piano: The Shard, London
- **Move [S]:** eight sloping, splayed glass facets that do not meet at the top; tapering form; mixed-use stack with residences high up.
- **Optimises:** less perceived bulk, a skyline silhouette, lighter weight.
- **Source:** https://www.rpbw.com/project/the-shard--london-bridge-tower

## B. Geometry tokens (48)

Format: **name**: description | parameters (typical range) | optimises | fails when | precedent / source.

Numeric ranges are **[H]** unless a source is given.

### Mass and plate (M)
1. **M.slender_square**: square plate with all four facades equal | side 20–30 m; slenderness H/B 6–24 (432 Park about 15 [S], 111 W57 about 24 [S], Kanchanjunga 4 [S]) | views on all sides, identity | wind motion grows with slenderness (needs dampers or blow-through floors); small plate means few units per floor | 432 Park, Kanchanjunga
2. **M.courtscraper_warp**: perimeter block with one corner raised and the opposite corner dropped | low corner 0–3 floors, peak 25–40 floors | opening the courtyard to the view and sun | small sites; costly geometry | VIA 57 West [S]
3. **M.height_gradient**: mass rises toward the side away from the view or sun | Δh across the site 5–40 floors | daylight and views over the lower parts | lower units lose views | 8 House [S], Waves 40→80 [S]
4. **M.plate_transform**: plate size or shape changes with program up the height | office depth 12–15 m to residential 7–10 m | right depth per use | transfer structure | Telus Sky [S]
5. **M.unfold_above_clearance**: small footprint up to a threshold height, then cantilever outward | threshold 20–40 m; cantilever 3–10 m cumulative | reclaiming area where views are best | walking columns and structural cost | Vancouver House [S]
6. **M.stacked_frustums**: alternate inverted and upright truncated pyramids | module 8–15 floors; face slope 1–3° | 8 corners per floor, varied orientation | curtain-wall complexity | St. Regis Chicago [S]
7. **M.twin_tower_offset**: two towers rotated or offset so they do not face each other | offset angle 15–45°; gap ≥ 20 m | less facing, more view corridor | site width | Imperial, Mumbai (twin towers [S]; offset reasoning [H])
8. **M.feathered_setback**: many small setbacks instead of a few large ones | 3–15 setbacks; step 0.5–2 m | follows the zoning envelope, gives a legible taper | little terrace depth | 111 W 57th [S]
9. **M.zoning_taper**: facets slope to follow a sky-exposure plane | slope set by the zoning ratio | maximum floor area within the envelope | awkward interiors | 53W53 [S]
10. **M.faceted_splay**: facades split into sloping, non-parallel planes | 4–8 facets; lean 1–5° | less perceived bulk, reflections, crown | sloped glazing costs more and leaks | The Shard [S]

### Rotation and twist (R)
11. **R.linear_twist**: constant rotation per floor | 0.5–2°/floor (Cayan 1.2° [S]); total 45–180° | spreads views, wind | vertical services must stay in a central core; balcony geometry changes every floor | Cayan [S]
12. **R.variable_twist**: rotation per floor varies with height | 1–8°/floor (Absolute [S]) | sculpted identity, view tuning by band | cost; units differ on every floor | Absolute World [S]
13. **R.plan_rotation_lightwell**: plan rotates so voids spiral | about 5°/floor [S] | daylight and ventilation into a deep plan | mostly an office precedent | 30 St Mary Axe [S]
14. **R.band_rotation**: rotate a block of N floors as a unit | N = 3–12; angle 5–30° | fewer unique floors than a continuous twist while still re-aiming views | transfer at each band | Vista/frustums, Telus Sky (by analogy [H])
15. **R.void_twist**: an internal atrium twists between two grids | 0–45° total [S] | reconciling grids | lost floor area | Leeza SOHO [S]

### Cantilever, stagger and pixel (C)
16. **C.slab_undulation**: slab edge offset varies floor to floor to aim views between buildings | edge offset 0–3.6 m [H]; each floor's outline is unique | views through gaps, shading, wind | formwork repetition drops | Aqua [S]
17. **C.stacked_cantilever**: whole-unit boxes shift out | 1–8 m offsets [H] | unique views, terraces | transfer structure; the cantilever underside is exposed | 56 Leonard [S]
18. **C.pixel_step**: grid of pixels removed to make terraces | pixel = 1 bay × 1 floor; 5–30 % removed | terraces, daylight, variety | waterproofing over habitable rooms | Valley [S], Telus Sky [S]
19. **C.stagger_alternate**: alternate floors shifted left or right | shift 1–3 m; period 2 floors | double-height terraces, privacy from above | elevational noise | Kanchanjunga split levels (analogous [S])
20. **C.twist_deck**: balcony or deck outline rotates around the unit per floor | 5–20°/floor for the deck only; the core stays straight | angled views from each unit | wind uplift on the deck | Puri unbuilt tower [S], Absolute [S]
21. **C.angled_balcony**: balcony front skewed toward a view | skew 10–45° | sea view from oblique plots, privacy from neighbours | smaller usable area | Puri Mumbai towers [S], Waves [S]

### Carving, voids and terraces (V)
22. **V.corner_cutout_double_height**: corner cut out to form a 2-storey garden | 2 floors × about 1–2 bays | view, rain and sun buffer, cross-ventilation | less area on premium corners | Kanchanjunga [S]
23. **V.sky_garden**: multi-storey communal void | 2–6 floors (Waves 6 levels [S]) | shared view, wind relief, landmark | lost area | Waves [S], Mirador [S]
24. **V.framed_view_void**: a large opening through the mass aimed at a landmark | void ≥ 3×3 bays | collective view, identity | wind acceleration through the void | Mirador [S], Morpheus [S]
25. **V.blow_through_floor**: open storey for wind | 1–2 floors per 20–40 floors [H] | wind, less sway | lost area | 432 Park [S], St. Regis [S]
26. **V.stepped_terrace_section**: terraces stepping toward the view | step 2–6 m per floor | a private garden for every unit, privacy (you look over roofs) | deep plans at the bottom | Mountain Dwellings [S], Habitat 67 [S], Doshi LIC [S]
27. **V.inverted_pyramid_units**: larger units low, smaller units above with terraces | step 1–3 m | adaptable terraces | overhangs shade the lower units | Doshi LIC [S]
28. **V.courtyard_open_to_view**: courtyard with one side opened toward the view or sun | opening 30–100 % of one side | shared views and daylight | privacy across the court | VIA 57 [S]

### Plan-edge tokens (P)
29. **P.chamfer_corner**: corner cut at 45° | chamfer 1.5–6 m [H] | widens the corner field of view, column-free corner room | less facade on the best corner | KPF Madison Square Park Tower [S/U]
30. **P.eight_corners**: plan shape with more corners (cruciform, frustum, star) | 6–12 corners | more corner units, which sell at a premium [S] | lower efficiency, more facade | St. Regis [S]
31. **P.splayed_wings**: wings diverge so windows do not face each other | splay 15–60° | privacy, views out | irregular rooms | Waves wings (linear arrangement [S]; splay values [H])

33. **P.single_unit_per_floor**: one apartment per floor | plate 250–600 m² | 360° view, total privacy, luxury | fewer units | EDEN [S]
34. **P.split_level_interlock**: units interlock across 1.5–2 floors | section interlock | double aspect, cross-ventilation, double-height space | accessibility | Kanchanjunga [S], 520 W28 [S]

### Relation and constraint tokens (X)
42. **X.facing_distance**: minimum window-to-window distance between habitable rooms | 18–21 m London yardstick [S]; Mumbai marginal open space H/4 capped at 12/16/20 m [S/U]; NYC 20 ft window to lot line [S] | privacy, daylight | can sterilise tight urban sites | London SPG, NYC ZR 23-37x
43. **X.view_corridor_alignment**: skew units toward a measured view cone | cone half-angle 15–60° | view share | the corridor may be built out later | Aqua [S], Waves [S]
44. **X.oblique_offset**: when facing distance is short, offset windows so the angle between facing windows is ≥ 30–45° | [H] | privacy at short distance | complicates the plan | London SPG allows "innovative design responses" [S]
45. **X.bridge_or_infrastructure_clearance**: setback radius from infrastructure | e.g. 30 m [S] | privacy and noise | loss of floor area | Vancouver House [S]
46. **X.sky_bridge**: link towers at intervals | every 10–15 floors (Sky Habitat 14/26/38 [S]) | shared amenity at height, structural coupling | wind | Sky Habitat [S]
47. **X.crown_articulation**: a specific move at the top (lean, taper, double-height glazing) | top 5–15 % of height | skyline identity | wasted area | Imperial [S], Shard [S], 111 W57 [S]
48. **X.base_podium_to_street**: podium that holds the street edge with the tower set back above | tower setback 10–15 ft (NYC [S]) | street life, wind at the base | podium units get poor views | NYC tower-on-a-base [S]

## D. City notes

### Mumbai
- **Orientation dilemma [S].** The sea and the best views lie west (plus harbour to the east). These are also the directions of hard sun and monsoon rain. The response is verandah or double-height garden buffers, fins, or angled decks.
  - https://charlescorreafoundation.org/2022/03/13/kanchanjunga/
  - Puri's Waves: south screens and a sea-facing north-west facade: https://worldarchitecture.org/architecture-projects/vnne/waves-project-pages.html
- **Marginal open space (DCPR 2034) [S/U].** Side and rear open space is about H/4 (minimum 3.6 m residential).
  - Capped at 12 m for buildings over 32 m up to 70 m, 16 m for over 70 m up to 120 m, and a flat 20 m above 120 m.
  - Projections up to 1.2 m are excluded.
  - One side needs 9 m of clear fire access, two sides above 120 m.
  - Minimum road width unlocks each height band (9 m → 70 m, 12 m → 120 m).
  - **These figures come from a third-party summary.** Verify against the official DCPR text: https://infralens.in/dcr/setbacks/mumbai ; official PDF: https://dtp.maharashtra.gov.in/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/MUMBAI-DCPR.pdf
  - Implication: tower-to-boundary gaps of 12–20 m are typical, so tower-to-tower facing distance on adjacent plots can be about 24–40 m. That is larger than London's yardstick, but the towers are taller.
- **Height limits from the airport funnel.** AAI Colour Coded Zoning Maps set permitted top elevations, and a NOCAS NOC is needed. The 2025 DCPR amendment lets unusable FSI in the funnel zone convert to TDR [S].
  - https://www.naredco.in/notification/pdfs/CCZM_Guidelines.pdf
  - https://indianexpress.com/article/explained/law-change-tdr-concession-mumbai-airport-9909476/
- **Fungible compensatory area:** 35 % residential [U: third-party summary]. It affects how balconies and decks count toward built area.
- **Market signal [U, marketing sources].**
  - Sea-view premiums of about 20–30 % are quoted for Worli and Marine Drive; up to 45–50 % for full views in Worli.
  - https://propertybutler.in/insights/marine-drive-marine-lines-residential-guide-2026 ; https://www.ghar.tv/intelligence/mumbai-coastal-road-real-estate-impact-2026-worli-micro-markets-outlook/artgi131
  - The peer-reviewed Hong Kong analogue is 11 % for partial and 22 % for full views: https://www.mdpi.com/2075-5309/16/9/1737
- **Local precedent moves:**
  - Kanchanjunga: corner double-height gardens.
  - Puri: angled or curvilinear balconies; every unit oriented to the sea; the largest units on the best corner.
  - Imperial: slender twin towers, full-height glazing.

### New York City
- **Height and setback [S].** In R8–R10 districts, a street wall rises to a maximum of 85 ft, then sets back 15 ft (wide street) or 20 ft (narrow street).
  - Above that, the sky exposure plane slopes 5.6:1 on wide streets and 2.7:1 on narrow streets.
  - Towers may penetrate the plane under tower rules: tower-on-a-base with 30–40 % lot coverage, tower setbacks of 10 ft (wide street) and 15 ft (narrow street), and a share of floor area below 150 ft.
  - https://zr.planning.nyc.gov/article-ii/chapter-3/23-736 ; https://www.nyc.gov/assets/planning/download/pdf/zoning/districts-tools/r10_tower.pdf
  - This is why many NYC towers show "feathered" or tapering profiles: 111 W 57th, 53W53.
- **Window distances [S].** A legally required window must be at least 20 ft from a wall or lot line, measured at the sill.
  - Separated portions of a building above 75 ft must be at least 30 ft apart.
  - https://zoningresolution.planning.nyc.gov/print/pdf/node/22765 ; https://up.codes/s/distance-between-legally-required-windows-and-lot-lines
- **Critiques.**
  - Towers-in-the-park superblocks cut residents off from the street (NYCHA): https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Towers_in_the_park
  - Billionaires' Row supertalls cast shadows on Central Park and are built as-of-right through air-rights assembly: https://www.architecturalrecord.com/articles/3071-community-forum-questions-crop-of-tall-buildings-around-central-park ; https://www.curbed.com/article/supertalls-scale-towers-central-park-height-view.html
- **View targets:** Central Park, the Hudson and East Rivers, the skyline (Empire State Building etc.). Precedents aim at these through height (432 Park) or through a warp or lift (VIA 57 toward the Hudson).

### London
- **Privacy.** The London Housing SPG uses **18–21 m between directly facing habitable-room windows** as a *yardstick*, not a rigid rule. It encourages design responses such as orientation and window placement where the distance cannot be met.
  - https://www.london.gov.uk/sites/default/files/housing_spg.pdf
  - Applied in appeals: https://acp.planninginspectorate.gov.uk/ViewDocument.aspx?fileid=53597201
  - Some boroughs cite 18 m: https://www.lbhf.gov.uk/sites/default/files/section_attachments/pg_spd_final.pdf
- **Protected views (LVMF / London Plan HC4).**
  - Protected vistas are made up of a Landmark Viewing Corridor and a Wider Setting Consultation Area.
  - Development above the threshold plane of the corridor should be refused.
  - The City of London's **St Paul's Heights** limits heights near the cathedral.
  - https://www.planningpolicy.co.uk/policy/london-plan/hc4 ; https://www.cityoflondon.gov.uk/assets/Services-Environment/planning-protected-views-tall-buildings-st-pauls-heights-study-part-1-2015.pdf
  - **Model these as hard exclusion volumes** (planes and wedges) in the optimiser.
- **Tall buildings policy (London Plan D9).** Assesses visual, functional, environmental (wind, daylight, sunlight, temperature) and cumulative impacts: https://www.london.gov.uk/sites/default/files/2025-05/Do%20tall%20buildings%20work%20in%20London.pdf
- **Daylight.** BRE 209: 25° rule; neighbour VSC ≥ 27 % or ≥ 0.8× the former value: https://bregroup.com/store/bookshop/site-layout-planning-for-daylight-and-sunlight-a-guide-to-good-practice-br-209-2022-edition
- **Wind.** Lawson criteria: sitting < 4 m/s, standing 4–6, strolling 6–8, business walking 8–10, uncomfortable > 10; safety threshold 15 m/s: https://data.london.gov.uk/download/2y55w/7279fbf9-132e-4842-acb9-8486332e8250/11.Wind.pdf
- **Heritage.** Historic England AN4 asks for coherence from all directions and minimal bulk at the top: https://historicengland.org.uk/images-books/publications/tall-buildings-advice-note-4/heag037-tall-buildings-v2/
- **View targets:** the Thames, St Paul's, the City cluster, the Shard. The Shard's splayed facets are the local precedent for reducing perceived bulk.

## E. Suggested defaults for the optimiser (all [H] unless noted)
- **Overlooking threshold:**
  - Hard fail below 12 m window-to-window for directly facing habitable rooms.
  - Soft penalty from 12 to 21 m.
  - No penalty above 21 m (London yardstick [S]).
  - Count windows as "facing" when the angle between their normals is within ±30–45°.
- **Rotation per floor:** 0–2° by default (Cayan 1.2° [S]); allow up to 8° in "sculptural" mode (Absolute [S]).
- **Cantilever per floor step:** ≤ 1.5 m without transfer; ≤ 3–4 m cumulative before a structural penalty. Larger values need a transfer or walking-column penalty (Vancouver House, 56 Leonard).
- **Chamfer:** 1.5–4 m. It adds roughly 10–30° of corner field of view depending on chamfer and room depth (geometric estimate; needs validation).
- **Corner unit weighting:** apply a multiplier above 1 (corner premium is shown by hedonic evidence [S]: https://ideas.repec.org/a/jre/issued/v35n32013p345-364.html). The magnitude depends on the market.
- **View value weights:** full sea view ≈ 1.0, partial ≈ 0.5, none ≈ 0. The ratio follows the HK hedonic 22 % vs 11 % [S].
