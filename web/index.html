    How it works

    1. Context comes from OpenStreetMap: buildings, streets, parks, water and coastline. Sea is traced from the coastline (OSM puts water on the right of the line). Building heights come from OSM height or building:levels × 3.2 m; otherwise they are estimated by building type and drawn lighter in 3D.
    2. The site is georeferenced to a latitude/longitude anchor and a local metre grid (x east, y north). DXF files can be placed with a reference point and true-north angle, or read directly as UTM coordinates.
    3. The surroundings are rasterised at 4 m and rays are cast every 2° to 3 km. View quality combines sea visibility (depression angle), distance to obstruction, open sky, parks and landmarks, minus a privacy penalty.
    4. Each typology is generated floor by floor. Options are checked against the hard rules, then views are evaluated on sampled floors in parallel Web Workers. The facade is split into units at its worst point, rooms take the best frontage, and each unit is classed with its reasons.
    5. Options are ranked by Pareto front without collapsing to one score. The top options are re-checked against future neighbours when the data includes them.

    Statutory rules are not modelled; you supply consumable FSI, maximum height and the buildable envelope. Low-confidence rule defaults (core size, depths, frontages) are assumptions for the design team to confirm. Rates are placeholders. The illustrative Dadar plot is not the project site.
