    What the engine does

    1. Rasterises the surroundings (2.5-D heights, sea, park) at 4 m and casts rays every 2° from observers on the envelope at 0, 25, 50, 75 and 100 m. It then finds the premium arc and the height where the sea view opens.
    2. Generates each typology per floor (square, rectangular, chamfered, diamond, curved, triangular, Y, T, cross, twisted, tapered, terraced), with core, perimeter columns and podium. Floors are added until max height or consumable FSI is reached.
    3. Rejects options that break a hard rule: inside envelope, FSI, height, core margin, core ratio, core-to-facade depth, slab span, overhang, room frontage, facing distance.
    4. Casts rays from every 2 m of facade (±75° window field, cosine-weighted) on sampled floors. The facade is split into units at its worst point so the sea side is shared by equal value. Living rooms and bedrooms take the best frontage.
    5. Classes each unit as premium, good, neutral or compromised with the measured reasons. Prices it, re-checks the top options against future neighbours, and ranks by Pareto front without collapsing to one score.

    This page runs a JavaScript port of the viewtower Python engine in your browser. Statutory regulations are deliberately not modelled. Rule defaults marked low-confidence in the repository (core size, depths, frontages) are assumptions for the design team to confirm. The sample site and prices are synthetic.
